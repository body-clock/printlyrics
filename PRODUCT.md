# Product

## Register

product

## Users

People who need clean, legible song lyrics on paper: musicians rehearsing, teachers preparing handouts, and listeners making personal reference sheets. They arrive with either copied lyrics or a supported source URL and expect to reach a useful print preview in one short flow.

## Product Purpose

PrintLyrics turns pasted or extracted song lyrics into a durable, shareable, print-optimized page. Success means the preview is trustworthy, the printed result is readable, and users can adjust type size and columns without learning a layout tool. A page can also be extended into a songbook: an ordered set of pages that prints as one job, which is what a rehearsal, a class period, or a service needs. The homepage and two practical guides explain printing; the songbook-saving page explains the account options for saving songbooks to reprint later, and sells the plans where the deployment can take a payment. These four surfaces are crawlable. Account-owned books require sign-in; guest and premium share links are unlisted, read-only where applicable, and noindex. Saved resources stay out of the sitemap.

## Account and Songbook Experiment

Accounts stay optional; search, manual entry, editing, preview, and printing
remain free without sign-in. A free account adds a cross-device songbook
library, claims the visitor's active guest songbooks on registration, and keeps
the same one-songbook-per-calendar-day allowance and 180-day retention window.

The proposed $3 monthly, $25 yearly, and $1 24-hour premium access removes the
daily songbook limit, preserves account-owned songbooks for reprinting, enables
revocable read-only share links while premium access is active, and prints
sheets without the code a free or guest sheet carries. Guest songbook links
remain unchanged. Where the deployment holds the Stripe values, the plans are
bought through Stripe Checkout and access is granted by Stripe's webhook rather
than by returning from the payment page; a host without them renders the plans
as the proposal they describe and takes no payment. Google sign-in is the one
third-party sign-in, optional beside the email and password form; no other
provider is part of this prototype.

The first product questions are whether free accounts return to their library,
whether premium users reprint or share sets, and whether read-only sharing helps
someone who receives a songbook. Measure those outcomes without sending account
identifiers, song titles, lyrics, or tokens. Do not add more paid features until
those behaviors show a repeated need.

## Brand Personality

Quiet, literate, and dependable. The product should feel considered without becoming precious, decorative, or self-conscious.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic SaaS landing pages, oversized marketing copy, decorative dashboards, glass effects, novelty controls, and interfaces that make the tool feel more complicated than printing a lyric sheet.

## Design Principles

1. Put the lyrics and the print result ahead of product chrome.
2. Make the shortest common workflow obvious without hiding useful control.
3. Earn trust by keeping preview and print behavior aligned.
4. Use familiar controls and plain language.
5. Keep the visual system calm enough for repeated practical use.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA for interactive surfaces, visible keyboard focus, semantic headings and labels, generous touch targets, reduced-motion compatibility, and color-independent status communication. Printed output must retain strong contrast without depending on screen theme.
